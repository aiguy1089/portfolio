## D. Strengths and limitations of selected NLP techniques (2–3 paragraphs)
The preprocessing pipeline applies lowercasing, punctuation/special character removal, stopword removal, tokenization, lemmatization (spaCy), and handling of missing/empty text, with source-specific normalization (e.g., URL cleanup). These steps reduce sparsity, normalize lexical variants, and remove low-information tokens, which typically improves the robustness of classical ML features like TF–IDF and can help shallow models generalize on small-to-medium datasets. Empirical studies have shown that well-chosen preprocessing can yield measurable performance gains for traditional classifiers; for example, evaluations report that careful normalization and tokenization improve downstream sentiment classification, while excessive or misordered steps may degrade results (e.g., removing sentiment-bearing tokens) (see e.g., Novielli et al., 2020; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon, 2022; Calefato et al., 2018).

However, the benefits are data- and model-dependent. Recent work shows that some steps, such as lemmatization, provide limited benefit for certain sentiment tasks, and that the order and selection of components matter (e.g., stopword removal before lemmatization may differ from the reverse). For modern transformer models, aggressive preprocessing may be unnecessary or even harmful because subword tokenizers already handle variation; nevertheless, for traditional pipelines using TF–IDF or classical ML, the selected steps remain effective, especially with moderate dataset sizes (>500) (Transformers and meta-tokenization, 2024; Evaluating the Effectiveness of Text Pre-Processing, 2022). In this project, given the rubric and the use of TF–IDF as an optional feature export, the selected techniques are appropriate and balanced.

## E. Rationale for dataset and preprocessing choices (2–3 paragraphs)
We selected the Kaggle Amazon Fine Food Reviews dataset due to its quality, availability, and strong alignment with product sentiment analysis. It contains explicit review text with associated numeric ratings, enabling reliable sentiment labeling via a transparent mapping (scores ≥4 positive, ≤2 negative, 3 neutral). The dataset’s scale supports sampling >500 entries to meet the rubric while remaining computationally tractable for preprocessing and optional feature extraction. This mirrors common industry practice for e-commerce NLP tasks and provides a representative domain for evaluating preprocessing impacts.

The preprocessing techniques were chosen to balance effectiveness and reproducibility for classical NLP workflows. Lowercasing, punctuation cleanup, and stopword removal reduce noise and sparsity; tokenization and lemmatization consolidate morphological variants to stabilize vocabulary; and URL/HTML removal addresses artifacts common in user-generated text. Prior studies indicate that these steps generally improve traditional sentiment pipelines, though benefits vary by model and language. Given our Python stack (pandas, spaCy, scikit-learn) and the project’s emphasis on preprocessing quality for sentiment analysis, this configuration supports reliable baselines, clear ablations, and straightforward documentation of decisions contributing to model effectiveness.
